# TOMA Coffee — Storefront, Brand & Blend Lab Strategy

> Working strategy document — consolidates the agreed direction, known brand facts, content approach, and product experience ideas so far.

## 1. The Core Idea

TOMA should not feel like a generic coffee shop or a catalogue with product cards.

It is a coffee brand built around:

- Good coffee, good vibes.
- Real coffee knowledge and approximately 12 years of hands-on experience.
- Freshly prepared coffee and carefully selected raw materials.
- Ready-made products for an easy purchase.
- A future **custom blend experience** where each customer can create coffee that matches their taste, routine, and goal.

The strongest positioning is:

> **TOMA does not only sell you coffee. It helps you build your coffee.**

Arabic supporting line:

> **قهوة على مزاجك، معمولة بخبرة TOMA.**

## 3. Website Role

The website is not only a CTA page. It is the homepage of a future online coffee store.

It must let a visitor quickly answer:

1. What does TOMA sell?
2. What is new?
3. How much does it cost?
4. Why is TOMA different?
5. Which coffee is right for me?
6. Can I buy a ready product or make my own blend?
7. How do I order, receive, and reorder it?

## 4. Visual Direction

### Brand mood

**Classic coffee heritage translated into a modern premium store.**

- Deep espresso brown and charcoal.
- Warm cream and parchment surfaces for product readability.
- Muted brass/gold as the one accent color.
- Editorial serif headings plus clean sans-serif UI, product details, and prices.
- Warm light, natural coffee texture, controlled depth, and restrained motion.

Avoid:

- Generic café-template visuals.
- Excessive rustic decoration.
- Flashy gradients or noisy animations.
- Dense text before products and prices.
- Fake reviews, countries, awards, or product claims.

### Hero art direction

The hero image is a **full-background campaign image**, not a separate card.

- A dark café table.
- One elegant brass serving tray.
- A dark coffee cup with visible crema and gentle steam.
- An open premium coffee container/bag.
- Only five to six large, distinct coffee beans falling into the container.
- Dark gradient overlay on the text side for readable copy.

## 8. The Differentiator: TOMA Blend Lab

### The promise

> **Your cup. Your signature. Guided by TOMA.**

TOMA Blend Lab gives a customer the right to create a coffee blend based on their taste and routine instead of only choosing from ready-made products.

The experience must not be an unrestricted ingredient form. The customer chooses preferences and outcomes; TOMA applies the expert rules, ratios, and limits required to protect quality.

### Customer journey

```text
Choose a goal or coffee moment
↓
Receive a TOMA starting recommendation
↓
Choose coffee style, strength, roast, and available additions
↓
See the blend build live
↓
Receive TOMA guidance and safe quality limits
↓
Name the blend
↓
See final price and packaging preview
↓
Add to bag, save it, or reorder later
```

### Blend builder inputs

These should be finalised only from ingredients that TOMA actually provides.

1. Coffee style / preparation
   - Turkish coffee.
   - Espresso.
   - Filter.
   - Any additional real formats.

2. Body and strength
   - Light and smooth.
   - Balanced.
   - Strong and concentrated.

3. Roast level
   - Light.
   - Medium.
   - Dark.

4. Aroma / addition
   - Plain.
   - Cardamom.
   - Other authentic additions.
   - Korean red ginseng where appropriate.

5. Pack size
   - 50g.
   - 100g.
   - 250g.
   - 500g.

6. Optional blend name
   - Example: “Morning Blend” or “خلطة الصبح”.

## 9. TOMA Guide: Suggestions, Advice & Limits

The guide is the element that makes the custom option feel like expert service rather than a generic configurator.

### What it does

- Explains the effect of each taste choice in plain language.
- Suggests a better next choice.
- Highlights a recommended range.
- Caps an ingredient at the quality limit set by TOMA.
- Explains why a certain combination is not recommended.
- Offers a better alternative instead of simply blocking the customer.

### Recommendation-zone UI

Every adjustable ingredient should have:

- A normal range.
- A visually highlighted `TOMA Recommended Range`.
- A `Maximum Balanced Amount`.
- A locked maximum that is defined by the coffee expert, not invented by the website.

Example guidance copy:

> **Cardamom adds a warm, eastern aroma.** Keep it light if you want the coffee itself to stay clear.

At the limit:

> **You reached the amount that keeps the cup balanced.** If you want a stronger character, TOMA can suggest a better coffee base or roast rather than hiding the coffee with more cardamom.

### Dynamic blend feedback

The system can say:

- “This blend is balanced for Turkish coffee.”
- “Your selection is now closer to a warm social cup than a strong workday cup.”
- “The current blend keeps your original goal, with a more eastern aroma.”
- “Would you like TOMA to suggest a more suitable version?”

## 10. Goal-Based Coffee Recommendations

The experience should begin with a human question:

> **What are you making coffee for today?**

Arabic version:

> **إنت عاوز قهوتك تعمل إيه في يومك؟**

### Suggested goal cards

- Start my day.
- Work and focus.
- Study / a long session.
- After lunch.
- Gathering and conversation.
- A quiet coffee ritual.
- Discover a new taste.
- I know what I like — build my own blend.

These are **taste and routine recommendations**, not medical claims. Avoid language such as “treats fatigue” or “medically improves concentration.”

### Recommendation result

```text
TOMA suggests: Turbo Mode

Fresh Egyptian Turkish coffee with Korean red ginseng.
We suggested it because you asked for a stronger, quick-preparation cup with
an authentic Turkish coffee character.

[ Buy Turbo Mode ] [ Customize this blend ] [ Show another option ]
```

### Recommendation logic

The first version does not need complex AI. It should use expert-approved rules.

Each product and base blend needs structured tags:

- Preparation method.
- Strength/body.
- Roast level.
- Aroma profile.
- Additions.
- Suitable coffee moments.
- Preparation time.
- Pack sizes and price.
- Availability by market.

The goal and the customer answers score eligible blends. The best match becomes a ready-made recommendation or a pre-filled custom blend.

## 11. Motion and Visual Interaction

Motion should be premium and informative, never cartoonish.

### When a customer adds an ingredient

- The ingredient moves from an ingredient shelf into the blending vessel.
- A brass measuring spoon or measured pour shows the quantity.
- The blend visual changes gradually.
- Aroma lines, steam, crema tone, or texture cues update subtly.
- The right-side blend summary and price update at the same time.

### Suggested builder layout

| Area | Role |
|---|---|
| Left | Steps and ingredient controls |
| Centre | Coffee/blend visual and subtle ingredient interaction |
| Right | TOMA Guide, flavour profile, final pack preview, price |

Avoid exaggerated particles or game-like effects. The visual language should feel like a refined coffee laboratory.

## 12. Three Customer Entry Points

The store should serve three kinds of customers:

| Customer | Entry point | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Wants a quick trusted option | Shop ready-made favourites | Buy a product such as Turbo Mode |
| Does not know what to choose | Help me choose | Goal-based recommendation and guided starting blend |
| Knows their taste | Build my own blend | Advanced guided customisation within TOMA rules |

## 13. Information Required Before Full Blend Lab Build

TOMA needs to define the operational truth behind the experience.

### Product and coffee rules

- Full list of available coffee beans and raw materials.
- Actual coffee styles and preparation formats.
- Available additions and ingredients.
- Minimum and maximum allowed amount for each addition.
- Ingredient combinations that should be blocked or discouraged.
- Coffee/roast combinations appropriate to each goal.
- Whether customers choose raw ratios or only taste outcomes.

### Pricing and operations

- Base price per pack size.
- Price effect of each ingredient or premium bean.
- Minimum custom order size.
- Custom-blend preparation time.
- Inventory rules and out-of-stock behaviour.
- Markets and delivery costs.
- Payment, cart, checkout, returns, and delivery policy.
- Whether a customer can print a custom name on the bag.

### Content and trust

- Real product photos for every live product.
- Real founder/roasting/coffee-lab images if available.
- Verified customer reviews with permission.
- Verified delivery markets and shipping times.
- Current contact channels for Egypt and U.S. sales.

## 14. Delivery Phases

### Phase 1 — Storefront MVP

- Homepage and real product catalogue.
- Product page for Turbo Mode.
- Real price, stock, and purchase/contact path.
- Goal quiz that recommends an existing product.
- Blend Lab teaser and waitlist/contact path.

### Phase 2 — Guided Blend Order

- Structured blend builder.
- Expert-approved limits and suggestions.
- Dynamic price.
- Blend summary added to cart.
- Manual TOMA review/confirmation where needed.

### Phase 3 — Full Blend Lab

- Saved customer profiles and saved blends.
- Reorder flow.
- Blend naming and packaging preview.
- Inventory-aware recommendations.
- Advanced/expert mode for customers who want finer control.

## 15. Final Product Principle

The website should always make the customer feel two things at the same time:

> **“I can buy something excellent immediately.”**

and

> **“If I know what I want, TOMA can make coffee that feels like mine.”**
